PART 1 — THE GOMEZ CASE (Santa Maria, 2021)
=====================================================================

July 10, 2021 — 11:15 p.m.
  A pedestrian — a Santa Maria man in his 60s — is crossing the 500
  block of North Broadway from east to west. A southbound vehicle
  driven by Alfonso Vazquez Gomez, 34, of Santa Maria strikes him. The
  impact throws the pedestrian into the northbound lane, where two
  more vehicles strike him. All three drivers flee.

July 10–14, 2021
  The SMPD Traffic Bureau investigates. It examines BOTH parties:
  the driver (Gomez), and the other party (the pedestrian). The
  investigation determines the pedestrian may have been at fault.

July 14, 2021
  Gomez is arrested and booked on suspicion of felony hit-and-run
  resulting in death, and driving on a suspended license.

September 7, 2021
  Gomez is charged with hit-and-run resulting in death and driving on
  a suspended license.

September 15, 2021
  Gomez pleads not guilty.

October 22, 2021
  Sgt. Michael McGehee, who runs the SMPD Traffic Bureau, states
  publicly that the pedestrian may have been at fault, and that Gomez
  might not have been charged had he stayed on scene. His words:
  "Just because a pedestrian has the right of way, they just can't
  walk out in front of traffic." Pedestrians "have to show due regard."

PART 4 — THE LEGAL ISSUES IN THIS TIMELINE, ADDRESSED
=====================================================================

A. COMMISSIONER AUTHORITY — THE MISSING STIPULATION

   THE RULE. Under article VI, section 21 of the California
   Constitution and Code of Civil Procedure section 259(d), a
   commissioner may act as a temporary judge only upon the
   stipulation of the parties. (Rooney v. Vermont Investment Corp.
   (1973) 10 Cal.3d 351.) A stipulation may be implied from conduct —
   the "tantamount stipulation" doctrine — but only where the party
   knew the officer was a commissioner and participated anyway. (In re
   Horton (1991) 54 Cal.3d 82, 99.) Where a self-represented party was
   never told the officer was a commissioner, no stipulation can be
   implied, and the rulings are void. (In re Marriage of Djulus (2017)
   10 Cal.App.5th 1042.)

   THE APPLICATION. Commissioner Carol Hubner presided at all three
   hearings (03/21/2024, 04/18/2024, 05/16/2024) and issued the
   Restraining Order After Hearing. The record shows no stipulation
   from either party. Both parties were self-represented. Nothing in
   the record shows either was advised of the officer's title or asked
   to agree.

   THE GAP. The minute orders. They will show whether a stipulation
   was taken on the record, and what was said about the officer's
   status.

B. MISSING PROOF OF SERVICE OF THE DVRO — THE KNOWLEDGE ELEMENT

   THE RULE. Penal Code section 273.6(a) is violated only by an
   "intentional and knowing violation" of a protective order. (People
   v. Faber (2017) 223 Cal.Rptr.3d 329.) Knowledge of the order is an
   element the People must prove.

   THE APPLICATION. The only proof of personal service in the file is
   dated 04/23/2024 — before the order of 05/16/2024 existed. No proof
   of service of the order itself appears in the file. Without service,
   the knowledge element is unproven.

   THE CAUTION. The account that deputies came to the residence and
   that Castillo refused the order is double-edged: it could be used to
   show he was told what the order was. It stays out of filed
   documents, and belongs in the defense analysis with counsel.

C. MENTAL HEALTH DIVERSION — CONSENT MUST BE KNOWING AND VOLUNTARY

   THE RULE. Penal Code section 1001.36, subdivisions (c)(2) and (3),
   require that the defendant consent to diversion, waive the right to
   a speedy trial, and agree to comply with treatment. A court may not
   deny diversion based on a subjective view of the defendant's
   "attitude" or "seriousness"; the court's residual discretion to
   deny diversion is limited to situations where the purposes of the
   statute would not be achieved. (Vaughn v. Superior Court (2024)
   (Cal. Ct. App., Sept. 17, 2024); People v. Whitmill (2022) 86
   Cal.App.5th 1138; People v. Brown (2024) 101 Cal.App.5th 113.)

   THE APPLICATION. The account describes papers put in front of
   Castillo without explanation, two days before a court date, and a
   court pressing him to sign. Consent produced that way is not knowing
   and voluntary. Diversion requires his consent. He has not given it.
